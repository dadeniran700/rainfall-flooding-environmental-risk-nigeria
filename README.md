# Rainfall, Flooding & Environmental Risk in Nigeria

## Project Overview

This project uses data analytics to explore historical rainfall patterns in Nigeria and investigate how annual rainfall relates to recorded flood events and reported flooded area.

The project combines long-term climate data with recorded flood-event data to identify patterns, relationships and limitations in understanding environmental risk.

## Research Question

How does annual rainfall relate to recorded flood events and reported flooded area in Nigeria?

## Objectives

- Analyse historical rainfall patterns in Nigeria from 1901–2025.
- Examine rainfall variability and long-term trends.
- Analyse recorded flood events from 1985–2022.
- Investigate the relationship between annual rainfall and recorded flood events.
- Investigate the relationship between annual rainfall and reported flooded area.
- Present the findings through an interactive Power BI dashboard.

## Data Sources

- World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal (CCKP) — historical precipitation data.
- Dartmouth Flood Observatory — recorded major flood events.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel — data cleaning, transformation and statistical analysis.
- Microsoft Power BI — data visualization and dashboard development.

## Key Findings

- Annual precipitation varied considerably between 1901 and 2025.
- The highest annual precipitation was **1,451.20 mm in 1957**.
- The lowest annual precipitation was **887.81 mm in 1983**.
- The linear rainfall trend had an R² of **0.0338**, indicating that the linear trend explains only a small proportion of annual variation.
- Between 1985 and 2022, the average number of recorded flood events was approximately **1.79 per year**.
- The highest number of recorded flood events occurred in **2003, with 9 events**.
- Annual rainfall had a positive relationship with recorded flood events (**r = 0.341**).
- Annual rainfall had a stronger positive relationship with reported flooded area (**r = 0.439; R² ≈ 19.3%**).
- The findings indicate that rainfall is an important factor, but rainfall alone does not explain flooding.

## Limitations

- The flood dataset represents recorded major flood events and does not capture every flood occurrence.
- Rainfall data is nationally aggregated and does not represent rainfall conditions at every location in Nigeria.
- Annual rainfall totals do not capture short-term rainfall intensity or location-specific extreme events.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
- Other factors such as drainage, land use, urban development, topography, river conditions and infrastructure were not fully modelled.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows substantial variability in Nigeria's historical rainfall and a positive relationship between annual rainfall and reported flood impacts during the common comparison period.

However, the results also show that rainfall alone cannot explain most of the variation in flooding. Understanding environmental risk therefore requires consideration of multiple climatic, environmental and human factors.
